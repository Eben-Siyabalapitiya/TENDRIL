01 What changed

Three things,
on purpose

The first arm worked, which is the only reason there is a second one. Phase one ended with a list of things it could not do, and the useful part of that list was short enough to actually attack.

Pick the things that were actually missing, not the things that were easy.

So V2 is not a redraw. It is the same spiral with three specific changes, and each one has a reason that came out of breaking the first one.

  1. 01 Axis

    A second axis

    V1 had one plane and one string, so it curled left and right and nothing else. V2 carries a second channel running at an angle to the first, which gives it a third direction to close in instead of being stuck flat.

  2. 02 Strength

    Stronger where it broke

    The first print snapped at the base, and the fillet fixed the crack without adding any material. V2 goes further and thickens the lower segments, because that joint carries the whole arm and now it carries a longer one.

  3. 03 Reach

    A longer arm

    More segments means more turns in the spiral, which means a wider outer loop and more surface touching the object. It also means more plastic for the base to hold up, which is the part that has to be tested rather than assumed.

02 The new direction

It closes a third way

Left and right were already there. The second channel adds a direction the first arm could not reach at all, so the arm can come at an object from somewhere other than straight on.

Two strings means two things to pull, and working out whether that is one hand or two is part of what this phase is for.

The arm prints in two halves because 354 mm will not fit on the bed in one go, and the two meet at a seam where both ends are the same 20.7 × 17.9 mm. That seam is the only joint in the whole thing that is not a living hinge.

The sketch above it is the same geometry solved live, not a video of one, drawn from the numbers in make_diagram.py. Eighteen joints folding 18° each is 324° of wrap by the time it is shut, whichever way it closes. The first channel gives the two directions V1 already had; the second one runs square to it, at 90°, and adds the third.

04 3D model parts

Four files,
one arm

What comes off the bed, and what each piece is for.

  • Tip half

    186.3 × 20.7 × 17.9 mm

    The thin end. Each segment is a shrunk copy of the one before it, down to a tip small enough that printing it any smaller stops being worth it.

  • Base half

    168.1 × 33.1 × 28.8 mm

    The end that holds everything up, and the end that snapped on V1. Fewer, fatter segments, and the one place where adding material actually matters.

  • Handle

    24.0 × 53.0 × 35.0 mm

    The manual adapter. No motor anywhere in this build, so this block and a finger are the whole actuator.

  • Guide rings

    100.0 × 30.0 × 15.0 mm

    Three rings on one plate, 20 mm bore. The string threads through these, which is what keeps it pinned against the body instead of pulling straight.

These are the reference parts this build started from, exported as STEP and tessellated for the browser. The V2 geometry is being drawn in Onshape and goes here once it prints.

05 Iterate log

What the
second one took

Phase one was finding out what the write-up left out. This one is finding out what the first arm left out.

  1. 01 Two channels, one body
  2. 02 Where the second one goes
  3. 03 Thickening the base
  4. 04 Adding segments
  5. 05 What it cost

  1. Two channels, one body

    The whole trick in V1 was that the string sat off to one side, so pulling folded the joints instead of squashing the arm. A second axis means a second channel with its own offset, in the same piece of plastic, without the two of them running into each other.

  2. Where the second one goes

    Lining one channel up through segments that are all different sizes already took a few goes. Doing it twice, at an angle to each other, in a part that still has to come off the bed in one piece, is the part of this phase that lives in CAD.

  3. Thickening the base

    The fillet stopped V1 cracking, but it did not add any plastic, it just stopped the load piling onto one line. A longer arm puts more on that joint, so the lower segments get thicker as well. Whether that was enough is a test, not an opinion.

  4. Adding segments

    Every segment is still a scaled copy of the one before it, so making the arm longer is a question of how many turns the spiral gets before the tip is too thin to be worth printing. More turns is more wrap, up to the point where the tip stops springing back.

  5. What it cost

    Longer and thicker means heavier and a longer print, and two channels means two strings to pull. None of that is free, and writing down what got worse is as much a part of this phase as writing down what got better.

06 Tests

Proving it got better,
not saying it did

Phase one ended with me knowing it worked because I watched it work. That is not enough to claim V2 is an improvement, so this is the part with numbers in it. These fill in as the tests get run.

How it gets measured

Printed test cylinders in a set of sizes, a fixed approach position so every attempt starts the same way, and ten tries per object instead of one good one. Grip gets measured by pulling the object back out and recording the force it let go at, which is the number that actually matters.

Against a frozen V1

A spare V1 printed and left unused, so there is something to compare against that has not been worn out by a term of demos. Same tests, same objects, same order. Anything V2 claims has to beat a number, not a memory.

07 Still missing

What V2 does not fix

Still pulled by hand

Two axes and a stronger base do not change the fact that a finger is the actuator. Every pull is a slightly different pull, which is a problem for a demo and a worse problem for a measurement. Driving it properly is the obvious next thing.

Still no idea how hard it grips

There is no sensing anywhere on the arm, so grip force is whatever my hand decided it was that time. Until there is something reading the tendon, the arm cannot hold a marble and a bottle at a known, equal force, which is the thing the spiral should be good at.

08 Where this is going

Three phases

This is the second one. The last one gets added here when it happens.

  1. Done

    Build

    Rebuild something that already exists and find out what gets left out when people write it up. Print it, break it, fix it, get it picking things up.

  2. Now

    Iterate

    Take the version that works and push on it. Change things, test what actually holds, and get it better than where it started.

  3. Next

    Use what the first two taught me to build something that is mine.
